# Arrow of Time Boundary  
*A Congruity 7.0 regime analysis*

## Overview
The arrow of time — the apparent directionality from past to future — is one of the deepest unresolved problems in physics.

Microscopic laws are largely time-symmetric, yet macroscopic processes exhibit irreversibility.

This repository reframes the arrow of time not as a fundamental force, but as a **boundary condition emerging from proportionality constraints**.

---

## Congruity Framing

Using the canonical Congruity relation:

\[
C = \frac{V}{E + I + S}
\]

the arrow of time appears when **information and structure costs accumulate asymmetrically across scales**.

- **E (Energy):** available free energy for state transitions  
- **I (Information):** entropy growth, loss of microstate accessibility  
- **S (Structure):** increasing structural complexity and constraint locking  
- **V (Functional value):** usable work, causal organization, persistence  

Time directionality emerges when I and S grow faster than E can compensate.

---

## Key Insight

Irreversibility does not require a time-asymmetric law.

It arises when reversing a process would demand **information and structural costs exceeding available energy**, breaking congruity.

The arrow of time is therefore a **congruence boundary**, not a primitive property of time itself.

---

## Entropy Reinterpreted

Entropy increase reflects not disorder, but **information cost inflation**.

As systems evolve:
- Accessible microstates shrink relative to macroscopic constraints
- Structural commitments accumulate
- Reversal paths become non-admissible under proportionality

Entropy growth marks loss of congruent reversibility.

---

## Implications

This framing suggests:
- No special initial condition is required to “start” time
- Time’s arrow is local, regime-dependent, and scale-sensitive
- Apparent time symmetry at microscopic levels coexists with macroscopic irreversibility

The arrow of time becomes a **diagnostic of coherence saturation**, not a mystery.
